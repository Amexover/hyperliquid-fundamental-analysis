### How this was built
- Cohort: the five projects the question named, resolved to Hyperliquid, Lighter, Aster, dYdX and GMX; the comparison is limited to these five, so the market-share denominators are the five-venue totals, not the whole market.
- Window: the last 30 days covered 2026-09-28 to 2026-10-27 and the last 90 days covered 2026-07-30 to 2026-10-27; the 30-day comparison is against the 30 days before that (2026-08-29 to 2026-09-27) and the 90-day comparison against the prior 90 days. Open interest compares the 2026-10-27 snapshot with the snapshots on 2026-09-27 (30-day) and 2026-07-29 (90-day).
- Values: fees, revenue, earnings, perp notional volume and open interest come from Token Terminal's project fundamentals; the project-level figures are each project's whole-project rollup.
- Fees are gross user-paid value and revenue is the retained portion before cost of revenue and operating expenses; they are separate measures and are not added together.
- HyperCore perps and HyperCore spot are the derivatives and exchange products respectively; USDC Reserve Income is a separate HyperCore-based product whose rows begin 2026-08-26; HyperEVM is the project's own chain product; Hyperliquid Outcome Markets is the prediction markets product. The product rows are the composition of the project-level total and must not be added to it (perps + spot + USDC reserve income + HyperEVM + outcome markets equal the project figure).
- Spot trading volume (not perp) was returned only at product level: Hyperliquid Spot $4.61B over 30 days and $15.20B over 90 days; Outcome Markets is a prediction markets product, not spot or perps, and is kept apart.
- Active users (daily) is the average of the daily revenue-generating-user counts over each window (counts of unique users are not summable, so the window is an average day); the requested perp-market "active traders" metric was not queried.
- Aster's derivative notional volume is reported off-chain (no on-chain venue row); all other venue and Hyperliquid figures are on-chain.

### Metric definitions

- **Fees** (`fees`): Fees measure the total value users pay to use a project’s application. Some examples include interest paid by borrowers (lending), trading fees paid by traders (DEX), and transaction fees paid by users (L1 & L2 blockchain). This metric shows how much the project is able to generate economic value.
  - Hyperliquid's own methodology: The total economic value paid by users to interact with the Hyperliquid ecosystem is measured by Fees. This metric includes trading fees paid by users on Hyperliquid Perps, Hyperliquid Spot, and Hyperliquid Outcome Markets, transaction gas fees paid on HyperEVM, and the gross yield generated on the USDC backing HyperCore under the Aligned Quote Asset V2 framework. These fees represent the total cost of utilizing the platform's trading and execution infrastructure, as well as the yield generated from reserve assets managed in partnership with Coinbase and Circle.
  - HyperEVM's own methodology: Fees measures the total value paid by users to execute transactions on HyperEVM. These fees are generated to cover the costs of computational resources, state transitions, and transaction execution on the network. The aggregation of these fees is utilized to evaluate the economic activity, user demand, and overall security budget of the HyperEVM execution environment. All transaction fees are denominated in the native gas asset and converted to USD to enable standardized analysis.
  - Lighter's own methodology: Fees measures the total value paid by users to execute trades on Lighter. This metric includes the sum of all maker and taker fees collected across all trading pairs and order books on the platform. These fees represent the total economic value generated by the exchange infrastructure and are used to assess the protocol's ability to monetize trading activity.
- **Revenue** (`revenue`): Revenue measures the portion of fees that a project retains, as determined by its take rate. The remaining fees are distributed to supply-side participants, such as lenders or liquidity providers. This metric shows how much the project earns from selling its product or service, before cost of revenue, token incentives, and operating expenses.
  - Hyperliquid's own methodology: Revenue measures the portion of fees retained by the Hyperliquid protocol after subtracting pass-through fees. Earnings from Hyperliquid Perps, Hyperliquid Spot, Hyperliquid Outcome Markets, HyperEVM gas fees, and USDC Reserve Income are captured by this metric. Pass-through fees, including builder-code tips and HIP-3 deployer cuts, are excluded from the revenue calculation as they are distributed directly to external frontends and asset deployers. For USDC Reserve Income, revenue is earned under the Aligned Quote Asset framework (AQA V2) from yield paid by the treasury deployer (Coinbase) on the USDC backing HyperCore.
  - HyperEVM's own methodology: Revenue measures the portion of fees that HyperEVM retains from its operations. For HyperEVM, revenue is calculated as the total gas fees paid by users to execute transactions on the network. Because no pass-through fees or external revenue-sharing agreements are applied to HyperEVM gas fees, 100% of the collected fees are retained as protocol revenue. This metric is utilized to assess the economic viability and self-sustainability of the HyperEVM execution environment.
  - Lighter's own methodology: The portion of fees retained by Lighter, as determined by the protocol's take rate, is measured by the Revenue metric. These earnings are derived from exchange activity on the order book platform, specifically encompassing both maker and taker fees. Since 100% of the fees generated on the Lighter chain are accrued to the protocol, revenue is equated to the total fees collected.
- **Earnings** (`earnings`): Earnings measures the net value a project retains after subtracting all expenses from its revenue. This includes cost of revenue, token incentives, and operating expenses, where applicable. Note that earnings reflects onchain profitability, and may understate true costs in instances where the project does not report its operating expenses onchain. Earnings are revenue minus the expenses a project reports (token incentives, cost of revenue, operating costs). Most projects report few costs beyond token incentives, so where earnings equal revenue the margin is 100% by construction: a gap in cost coverage, not a measure of profitability.
  - Lighter's own methodology: Earnings represents the net financial performance of Lighter DEX, calculated as the difference between total revenue and total expenses. This metric answers the fundamental business question of whether the protocol generates positive or negative cash flow from its operations. For Lighter DEX, the current methodology reflects a protocol that operates with zero fees, no token incentive expenses, resulting in zero earnings.
- **Notional trading volume** (`notional_trading_volume`): Notional trading volume measures the total value of derivative contracts executed on a decentralized derivatives exchange. Since users pay trading fees on each trade, this metric tends to correlate with the project's ability to generate fees and revenue.
- **Open interest** (`open_interest`): Open interest measures the total USD notional value of all open positions on a derivatives protocol at a point in time. It is a leading indicator of speculative activity and capital at risk, complementary to trading volume. Open interest excludes positions that have already been closed or liquidated within the period.
- **Active users (daily)** (`user_dau`): Active users (daily) measures the number of unique addresses that make a revenue-generating transaction with a project, i.e. an application or a blockchain. Daily active users are measured over a fixed 24-hour window.
- **Trading volume** (`trading_volume`): Trading volume measures the total value of trades executed on a decentralized spot exchange. Since users have to pay trading fees on their trades, this metric tends to correlate with the DEX project's ability to generate fees and revenue.
